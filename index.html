partials of the instrument

hold a pad or its key to sustain. click a remembered shape to pin it, and it stops fading and keeps coming back — under the fixed-root engine a pinned shape also counts as home wherever the music is, so it can arrive in the middle of anything. the small numbers are how long each note is held. nothing anywhere rounds a pitch to a fixed step: every note you hear is the exact ratio printed on its pad. under each ratio is how far above the root it sits. how brightly a pad is drawn is the other thing worth knowing about it — how simply it relates to that root, so the plain ones come forward and the far-flung ones recede. hover a pad for the number. the field above is those same ratios as waves, one each, running the way its prime points and as fine as it is far from home; what you see is where they cross. try d then 2, or s then w: each pair is a comma apart — two different ways of reaching almost the same place, which stay stubbornly different, and here stay that way. hold one of those pairs and watch the field come back round: it takes the better part of a minute, where the root and 3/2 take under two seconds. perform gives the whole page to that same picture with the eye inside it rather than looking at a slice of it — flying forward at one panel width a second, which is the only speed at which what is ahead of you is what this panel would be showing. so the wait you just sat through is a distance instead: the comma pair is a long tunnel and 3/2 a short one. escape comes back here.
